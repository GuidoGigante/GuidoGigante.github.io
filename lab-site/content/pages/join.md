We are usually looking for people, and the honest answer about what we
have funding for changes through the year. If you are interested, write —
worst case we tell you to try again in six months.

## What we have open right now

**PhD, starting autumn 2026.** One funded position on the cross-species
comparison project. Suits someone with a quantitative background who is
willing to learn the biology, or a biologist who is willing to learn to
program properly.

**Masters projects.** Two or three per year, usually on the imaging
pipeline. Get in touch by March for an autumn start.

**Postdocs.** Nothing funded at the moment, but we are happy to support
fellowship applications and we have done so successfully four times. Start
that conversation early — most schemes want a draft six months ahead.

## What to send

An email to [lab@example.edu](mailto:lab@example.edu) with a CV and a few
paragraphs about what you want to work on. The paragraphs matter more than
the CV. If you have read one of our papers and disagree with it, say so.

You do not need to have published before. You do not need to already know
the techniques. You do need to be able to write code that other people can
read, or to want to learn how.

## How we work

Everyone in the lab writes code and everyone touches data. We use Python,
Git and code review, including for analysis scripts. Meetings are Tuesday
mornings; the rest of the week is yours to organise.

We do not expect evenings or weekends. Fieldwork and imaging runs
occasionally break that, and we plan around them rather than pretending
they do not happen.
