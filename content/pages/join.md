We are always looking for sharp, driven people. The honest answer about what we have funded changes throughout the year, but if you are interested in what we do, write to us. Worst case, we tell you to try again in a few months.

## What we have open right now

**Postdoctoral Researcher (2 years): Machine Learning / GeoAI.** We are looking for an expert in ML/DL with a strong quantitative background (Physics, Mathematics, Statistics, Data Science, or similar "hard" disciplines). You will be the primary computational driver for **DARDO**, our disease-agnostic prediction system, with crossover into our Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR) network models (Mutagraph). We expect strong proactivity and the ability to work independently.

> **About DARDO:** DARDO is a "GeoAI by design" pipeline for syndromic surveillance. It anticipates respiratory outbreaks days before emergency rooms surge by fusing structured participatory data (e.g., InfluWeb) with unstructured social media signals. You will work on its core architecture: a Spatial-Then-Temporal Graph Neural Network (ST-GNN) that uses fuzzy spatial catchments, deep sequence models (RNNs/LSTMs), and dual-regularization to separate genuine epidemiological signals from spatial white noise.

**Masters and PhD projects.** We host a few students per year, usually focusing on network dynamics, time-series analysis, or XAI. There are no fixed deadlines—get in touch whenever you want to discuss a potential project. 

## What to send

Send an email to [guido.gigante@iss.it](mailto:guido.gigante@iss.it) with your CV and a few paragraphs about why you want to work with us. The paragraphs matter more than the CV. If you have read one of our papers and disagree with it, say so. 

We do not expect you to already know every technique we use, but we do require a specific mindset. You must be able to think deeply about problems from multiple points of view. We sit at the intersection of basic science and real-world impact—between theoretical models and public health. 

We believe that effective innovation thrives at the intersection of broad curiosity and strict constraints. We will encourage you to cultivate a diverse mental library—drawing insights and lateral analogies from widely unrelated disciplines—but we will also challenge you to execute those ideas within the boundaries of limited resources and real-world utility. This discipline means you won't just write algorithms in a vacuum; you will consider a problem across its entire lifespan, from its most basic mathematical formulation through to software usability, real-world testing, and the systemic barriers of clinical adoption.

## How we work

**The tools:** Everyone in the lab writes code and everyone touches data. We use Python heavily, but not exclusively—we use whatever tool best solves the problem.

**The schedule:** We value deep work and independence. We do not have fixed, mandatory lab meetings. You organize your own week. After your first three months, we support 2–3 days a week of remote working. We do not expect you to work evenings or weekends; while major grant or publication deadlines occasionally break that rule, we plan around them rather than pretending they don't happen.

**The culture:** We foster an inclusive, friendly, but intellectually rigorous scientific culture—a place of healthy, spirited debate. (The ISS as an institute has strong female representation, and we actively champion diversity).
We strive to build a team where individuals are celebrated for being wonderfully asymmetric in their talents. We want an environment that maximizes everyone's unique momentum: the specific trajectory and speed of their personal growth.
In practice, this means we actively look for opportunities to catch each other doing things right, using these moments of success to accelerate natural development. We encourage every team member to identify the specific tasks that bring them genuine joy, energy, and a sense of flow—whether that is wrestling with a complex math problem, writing clean Python code, or designing a beautiful plot—and we try to weave those activities into their core responsibilities. However, true growth requires friction. Because we anchor you in your strengths, we will also encourage you to step out of your comfort zone and take intellectual risks.
We strive to create a place of deep trust and psychological safety, where everyone feels completely secure in expressing unconventional views. By valuing distinct strengths and providing the safety to stretch beyond our limits, we can rapidly adapt and solve fast-paced, real-world problems together.
